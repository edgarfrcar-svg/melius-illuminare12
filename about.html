About Melius Illuminare

A cinematic digital encyclopedia of Catholic theology — the Fathers as received by the Church, the Doctors, the Scholastics, and the great schools of Catholic spiritual formation. The tone is that of a museum exhibit, a historical documentary, and a sacred archive: dramatic, reverent, and visually rich.

Editorial Posture

All biographical, doctrinal, and summary content on this site is written specifically for Melius Illuminare. It is not scraped or lightly reworded from other websites. Sources include public-domain primary texts, classical Catholic scholarship, and the living reception of the Fathers and Doctors within the Catholic tradition.

The site is intentionally Roman Catholic in scope. Figures and spiritualities are included when they belong to the Catholic Church’s living tradition or to the undivided Church of the first millennium as received by Catholicism. Protestant reformers and purely post-Schism Orthodox writers are omitted so that the encyclopedia can present Catholic doctrine with depth and coherence.

Images — Strict Public-Domain Rule

No AI-generated images appear anywhere on this site.

Every portrait, icon, and artwork is drawn from verifiable public-domain sources only:

  • Wikimedia Commons (files tagged PD-old, PD-art, or equivalent)
  • Web Gallery of Art
  • Internet Archive
  • Library of Congress
  • Yorck Project scans and similar pre-1929 photographic reproductions of older works

Each image carries (or will carry) a small attribution noting the holding institution or Commons file and its public-domain status. If a portrait has not yet been sourced, a tradition-colored monogram is shown instead of a photograph.

Texts — Reproduction Rules

Full text is reproduced on-site only when the work is genuinely in the public domain. Primary sources are drawn especially from:

Catholic Library, New Advent (Fathers, Summa, Catholic Encyclopedia), CCEL (ANF / NPNF series), and verified Project Gutenberg editions of the Doctors and Scholastics.

For any work that is not public domain (most 20th- and 21st-century theology, most post-1929 magisterial documents), the site shows an original summary plus an outbound “Read at [Source]” link with attribution. We never reproduce copyrighted text.

Bible Translations

The Sacred Texts library prioritizes public-domain translations long used in Catholic study:

  • Douay-Rheims
  • King James Version (1611) — for comparative reading of the shared text
  • American Standard Version (1901)

All of the above are public domain. Chapter-by-chapter readers will expand as content is added.

Tradition Color Coding

Early Church (pre-1054) — bronze Catholic — gold

Figures of the undivided Church receive the early-church bronze ring. Later Catholic Doctors, Scholastics, and mystics are marked gold. The encyclopedia presents both as belonging to the one Catholic tradition.

Contact & Corrections

This is an expanding archive. If you notice a factual error, a missing attribution, or a portrait that can be replaced with a better-documented public-domain image, the content will be corrected. The goal is accuracy and reverence, not speed.
